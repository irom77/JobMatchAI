# Massive Droner 3-inch ducted guard notes

Compatibility notes for fitting Stan FPV's 3-inch Universal Ducted Propeller Guards to the Catalyst Machineworks Massive Droner 3-inch HD frame.

## Fit and mounting

- The Massive Droner 3-inch HD listing specifies M2 motor mounting holes on a 9 mm or 12 mm diameter bolt circle. The guard listing specifies compatibility with 9–12 mm, four-bolt M2 motor patterns, so the listed frame pattern matches.
- The guards mount underneath each arm, using the motor mounting bolts. They do not go between the arm and motor.
- The included 1 mm and 2 mm rubber spacers go between the arm and guard to adjust clearance and dampen vibration.
- Longer motor bolts are required. Stan FPV estimates 2 mm longer with no spacer, or 4 mm longer with a 2 mm spacer; check the actual stack thickness and ensure the bolts do not bottom out against the motor windings.

## Propellers

The guards use 2.5-inch props in place of the frame's normal 3-inch props. Stan FPV recommends the HQProp T2.5x2.5x3 or an equivalent. The T-mount version fits motors with a 1.5 mm shaft / T-mount. If the installed motors use a 5 mm shaft, the seller says a 3-inch prop must be trimmed to 2.5 inches; check the resulting clearance and balance carefully.

## Installation cautions

- Do not use thread locker on the injected guards; Stan FPV warns it can damage the guard material. If thread locker has been used on the bolts, allow it to dry fully before installing them.
- Confirm each prop spins freely inside its duct and does not contact the guard before powering up.

## Betaflight Crash Recovery

Adding the guards alone is not a reason to enable Betaflight Crash Recovery. Leave it off initially. Betaflight says this feature is designed for whoops and smaller craft and warns pilots to test it carefully on larger, open-prop quads. A guarded Massive Droner falls between those cases, so behavior should be evaluated cautiously in a wide-open area before relying on it. Crash Recovery attempts to level the craft after detecting a crash, then returns control to the pilot; guards do not guarantee a safe recovery.
